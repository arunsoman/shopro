# Menu Engineering — Recommended Actions

> Based on the Menu Engineering Study (April 2026). Restaurants that apply these principles consistently see a **10–15% increase in profits within 90 days**, with some achieving up to 35% revenue increases through optimized menu design.

---

## 1. Classification-Driven Actions (The 4 R's)

| Classification | Root Cause | Action | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **⭐ Stars** — High Profit + High Popularity | Already performing well | **Retain** — Protect quality, feature prominently on menu, use in marketing, consider modest price increases ($0.50–$1.00) | Maintain |
| **🧩 Puzzles** — High Profit + Low Popularity | Product is good, marketing is bad | **Replate** — Add photos, improve descriptions, rename, move to Golden Triangle, train servers to recommend | **High** |
| **🐴 Plow Horses** — Low Profit + High Popularity | Priced too low relative to food cost | **Reprice** — Raise price $1–2, reduce portions slightly (10% often goes unnoticed), substitute cheaper ingredients, bundle with high-margin sides/drinks | **High** |
| **🐶 Dogs** — Low Profit + Low Popularity | Neither sells well nor earns margin | **Rethink** — Remove entirely if no strategic purpose, hide at bottom of section, reinvent recipe, or replace with new Puzzle candidates | **Immediate** |

### Quick Reference: The 4 R's Action Guide

| Category | Primary Action | Specific Tactics |
|---|---|---|
| **Stars** | RETAIN | Feature in Golden Triangle, maintain exact recipe specs, use in marketing, consider $0.50–$1.00 price increases, never discount, monitor ingredient costs closely |
| **Puzzles** | REPLATE | Add photos (1–2 per page max), improve descriptions with sensory language, rename with appealing titles, move to high-visibility positions, train servers with specific recommendation scripts, test as limited-time specials, lower price slightly if margin allows |
| **Plow Horses** | REPRICE | Increase price $1–2 gradually, reduce portion size ~10%, substitute cheaper ingredients where quality impact is minimal, bundle with high-margin sides or drinks, move to less prominent positions, add premium upcharge options (e.g., +$5 for extra protein) |
| **Dogs** | RETHINK | Remove entirely if no strategic purpose, hide at bottom of section with minimal description, evaluate for dietary restrictions or variety needs, check if acting as price anchor, rework recipe and reprice if potential exists, replace with new Puzzle candidates |

## 2. Menu Design Actions

> Eye-tracking studies reveal the **Golden Triangle** pattern: customers scan menus in this order — **Center → Upper Right → Upper Left**.

| Action | Expected Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Golden Triangle placement** — put Stars & Puzzles in center, upper-left, and upper-right positions | ↑ 20–30% orders on repositioned items | Most effective single change |
| **Anchor pricing** — place the most expensive item at the top of each menu category section | Makes mid-priced items feel like great value (decoy effect) | Expensive item doesn't need to sell well |
| **Descriptive language** — rewrite descriptions with sensory words (crispy, slow-roasted, heritage, cedar-plank, velvety) | ↑ 27% sales on described items | Cornell University study |
| **Add photography** to Puzzles (1–2 per page maximum) | ↑ 30% orders on photographed items | Too many photos signals "low-end"; reserve for high-margin Puzzles |
| **Box/highlight** Stars and Puzzles with callout boxes or icons ("Chef's Favorite", "Most Popular", "House Special") | ↑ 2–3x more attention than plain listings | Never highlight Plow Horses or Dogs |
| **Remove "$" signs** from all prices | ↑ 8% average spend | Cornell study — dollar sign activates "pain of paying" |
| **Nest prices** at the end of descriptions, not aligned in a column | Prevents customers defaulting to cheapest option | Vary price placement; avoid vertical price alignment |
| **Decoy strategy** — place a slightly overpriced item next to your target Star | Makes the Star feel like better value | The decoy doesn't need to sell; its job is to anchor perception |
| **Color psychology** — use red/yellow for callouts, green for freshness signals, orange to stimulate appetite | Complements positioning | Avoid blue for food items (suppresses appetite) |
| **Strategic use of white space** | Signals premium quality | Don't overcrowd item descriptions |

## 3. Pricing Actions

| Item Type | Price Action | Specific Guidance | Warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stars** | **Increase price $0.50–$1.00 gradually** | Small increases across multiple Stars compound to significant annual impact. Test on one item first. | Customers who order repeatedly are least price-sensitive — they've already self-selected |
| **Plow Horses** | **Raise price $1–2 and reduce portions 10%** | Can be done together or separately. Monitor sales volume for 4–6 weeks after changes. | Risk of customer pushback — do gradually and explain quality changes if asked |
| **Dogs** | **Remove or completely reinvent** | If keeping for dietary/variety, reprice to at least cover plate cost with minimum margin | Check if acting as price anchor before removing |
| **New Puzzle candidates** | **Enter at optimal price point** | Price to achieve >60% contribution margin; avoid entering at Plow Horse range | |
| **All fine dining items** | **Use round numbers** ($24, $42) | Signals premium quality | |
| **All casual/fast casual items** | **Use charm pricing** ($12.95, $9.99) | Signals value | |
| **Beverages** | **Apply same pricing framework** | Cocktails, wine, specialty drinks — use engineering framework | |

### Price Increase ROI Example

- Star selling 40 units/day
- $0.75 price increase
- Annual additional profit: **$10,950**

## 4. Kitchen & Plate Cost Actions

| Action | Benefit | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Bulk purchasing** to reduce per-ingredient plate cost | Raises contribution margins across entire menu | Negotiate with suppliers; buy seasonal in bulk and freeze |
| **Substitute cheaper ingredients** on Plow Horses where quality impact is minimal | Reclaim margin without raising prices | Test changes with small batches; monitor customer feedback |
| **Standardize recipes** for Stars — exact ingredient specs | Protects margin + ensures quality consistency | Create recipe cards; kitchen should not improvise on Stars |
| **Include ALL ingredients in plate cost** — garnishes, sauces, bread, fries, oils, seasonings | Accurate margin calculation | Missing these understates true plate cost |
| **Bundle Plow Horses** with high-margin sides and drinks | Raise effective margin without raising dish price | E.g., burger + fries + soda bundle |
| **Add premium upcharges** to Plow Horses | Increase average ticket margin | "+$5 for double patty", "+$3 for truffle fries" |
| **Calculate plate costs when supplier prices change** | Prevent margin erosion | Trigger: whenever supplier notifies of price change |
| **Identify and eliminate waste** on low-margin items | Direct profit improvement | Track prep waste per item |

## 5. Process Actions (Quarterly Cadence)

| When | Action | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly** | Pull sales mix report from POS | Track which items are selling and which aren't |
| **Monthly** | Monitor ingredient price changes; recalculate plate costs | Prevent margin erosion from cost increases |
| **Quarterly** | Replot full matrix — categories shift as costs and preferences change | Keep menu aligned with current data |
| **Quarterly** | Rewrite descriptions for changed/new items | Maintain descriptive language benefits |
| **Quarterly** | Review and adjust target distribution | Healthy range: Stars 20–25%, Puzzles 15–20%, Plow Horses 25–35%, Dogs <15% |
| **Before every menu change** | Train servers on new recommendations and scripts | Ensure front-of-house executes strategy |
| **4–6 weeks after changes** | Analyze results: compare sales mix, food cost %, average check size | Validate that changes are working |
| **Seasonally** | Refresh menu with seasonal ingredients | Lower costs when in season; keep menu exciting |

## 6. Staff & Training Actions

> Best menu = failed if servers don't recommend correctly.

| Action | Script / Approach |
|---|---|
| **Train servers to recommend Puzzles by name** | *"The [Puzzle item] is incredible tonight — chef uses a 45-minute reduction that makes the sauce unbelievable."* |
| **Upsell Stars to every table** | Pair with appropriate wine, side dish, or dessert |
| **Create incentive programs** tied to high-margin item sales | Monthly contest for server with highest margin sales mix |
| **Share item-level margin data** with servers | They become intelligent recommenders who understand what drives the business |
| **Role-play conversation practice** | Practice scripts for hesitant customers |
| **Teach the Golden Triangle** to servers | Help them understand why they should push certain items |
| **Don't force recommendations** — teach storytelling | Customers respond better to genuine enthusiasm than scripted pitches |

## 7. Digital & Online Menu Actions

> With **42% of orders** starting on mobile, digital menus are as important as printed ones.

| Action | Details |
|---|---|
| **Apply Golden Triangle to online/QR code menus** | Same positioning logic, different canvas |
| **A/B test descriptions** on digital menus | Different photos, different copy — quick to update |
| **Ensure mobile-friendly formatting** | Thumb-scannable, large prices, clear item names |
| **Sync digital menu pricing with printed menu in real-time** | Prevent margin leakage from delayed price updates |
| **Use highlight/box styling** for Stars and Puzzles in digital layout | Digital callout boxes work the same as printed |
| **Test different item order** in digital menus | Algorithm testing for conversion optimization |
| **Feature digital-exclusive deals** on Puzzles | Drive trial of high-margin items |

## 8. Beverage Menu Engineering

> Beverages typically carry **75–85% margins** — often higher margin than the best entrée. Apply the same framework.

| Action | Why |
|---|---|
| Feature high-margin cocktails in Golden Triangle positions | $9 cocktail at $1.80 cost = $7.20 margin |
| Bundle beverages with Plow Horse entrees | Raise overall check margin |
| Engineer the wine list using same principles | Stars (popular + profitable), Puzzles (profitable but slow sellers) |
| Train bar staff on upselling premium spirits | High-margin upsell opportunity |
| Consider house-made syrups and mixers | Reduce beverage cost further; 85%+ margins achievable |
| Use decoy pricing on drink menus | Expensive cocktail makes mid-priced cocktails feel reasonable |

## 9. Strategic Menu Configuration Targets

| Classification | Healthy Range | Action if Outside Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Stars** | 20–25% of menu items | If too few → promote more Puzzles toward Star status; if too many → may be cannibalizing variety |
| **Puzzles** | 15–20% | If too many → visibility problem is systemic; every Puzzle should have a clear promotion plan |
| **Plow Horses** | 25–35% | If too many → repricing is urgent; these items fund the business but drain margin |
| **Dogs** | <15% (target) | Anything above 20% means significant margin is being wasted; immediate action needed |

## 11. Pre-emptive Checklist: Mistakes to Avoid

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Action |
|---|---|
| Removing Plow Horses immediately | Reprice first — they drive customer loyalty and traffic |
| Using food cost % instead of contribution margin | Always use dollar CM ($13 margin beats 25% FC every time) |
| Overcrowding menu (>10 items per category) | Simplify → decision fatigue → customers pick cheapest familiar option |
| One-time menu engineering analysis | Make it quarterly — costs change, preferences shift |
| Ignoring seasonality | Refresh quarterly with seasonal ingredients + lower costs |
| Adding too many photos | Signals "low-end"; 1–2 per page maximum |
| Aligning prices in a vertical column | Customers scan column and pick cheapest — nest prices in descriptions |
| Engineering once and forgetting | Monitor continuously; automate where possible |
| Training staff on general upselling | Train specifically on **Puzzles** — generic upselling misses the point |
| Keeping items that are both Dogs and price anchors | Evaluate if the anchor role justifies keeping it |

## 12. 90-Day Implementation Roadmap

```
WEEK 1–2
  ├── Pull 90 days of POS sales data
  ├── Calculate plate costs for all menu items
  ├── Calculate contribution margin per item
  ├── Plot all items on Menu Engineering Matrix
  └── Categorize into Stars, Puzzles, Plow Horses, Dogs

WEEK 2–3
  ├── Rewrite descriptions for top 10 high-margin items
  │   └── Add sensory language, origin/quality descriptors
  └── Review current menu positions against Golden Triangle

WEEK 3–4
  ├── Re-position Stars & Puzzles to Golden Triangle positions
  ├── Add box/highlight callouts to Stars
  └── Implement anchor pricing at top of each section

WEEK 5–6
  ├── Test $0.50–$1.00 price increase on 2–3 Stars
  │   └── Monitor volume for 4 weeks
  └── Begin repricing 1–2 Plow Horses ($1–2 increase)

WEEK 7–8
  ├── Add photography to 2–3 Puzzles (1–2 per page)
  ├── Train all servers on Puzzle recommendation scripts
  └── Implement incentive program for high-margin sales

WEEK 9–10
  ├── Remove or reinvent 1–2 Dogs
  │   └── Replace with new Puzzle candidate at optimal price
  └── Test portion reduction on highest-volume Plow Horse

WEEK 11–12
  ├── Re-analyze: compare current matrix against baseline
  │   ├── Sales mix change
  │   ├── Average check size
  │   ├── Food cost %
  │   └── Profit per item vs. 90 days ago
  └── Plan Q2 changes based on results
```
